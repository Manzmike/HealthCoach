# How to find your numbers — practical methods

Plain-language reference for the personal metrics a schedule relies on. These are standard
sports-medicine / nutrition methods. `build_schedule.py` injects this file as the authoritative
method reference so the plan explains each term correctly; the library's graded papers still
supply the evidence and any A/B/C ratings. Nothing here is a drug/supplement dose.

## Zone 2 (easy aerobic base)
**What it is:** the highest effort you can sustain while staying truly aerobic — roughly the
top of "easy." Builds the aerobic engine, fat oxidation, and mitochondria; the bulk of
marathon volume should live here.
**How to find yours (cheapest first):**
1. **Talk test** — you can speak full sentences, only slightly labored. If you can only get a
   few words out, you're too high.
2. **Nose-breathing test** — you can breathe through your nose the whole time. Mouth-gasping = too high.
3. **% of max HR** — estimate max HR = **208 − 0.7 × age** (Tanaka; more accurate than 220−age),
   then Zone 2 ≈ **60–70% of max**. (Age 27 → max ≈ 189 → Zone 2 ≈ 113–132 bpm.)
4. **MAF method** — a simple aerobic ceiling of **180 − age** bpm (age 27 → ~153 bpm cap).
5. **Lactate meter (~2 mmol/L)** or a lab test — most precise; optional.
**Verify:** at a steady Zone-2 effort your HR drifts up only slightly (<5–10 bpm) over 30 min;
you finish feeling like you could keep going.

## Max heart rate
**Estimate:** **208 − 0.7 × age** bpm. Only a starting point — a real max effort (or a lab test)
is more accurate. Use it to anchor the % zones above.

## RPE and reps-in-reserve (RIR)
**RPE (1–10):** how hard an effort *feels* — 10 = maximal, 7 = "could do ~3 more reps."
**RIR:** reps left in the tank at the end of a set (RIR 2 = stopped 2 reps short of failure).
**Targets:** for muscle growth, most working sets at **RPE 7–9 / 1–3 RIR**; only occasionally to failure.
**Find yours:** on a set, honestly ask "how many more clean reps could I have done?" — that number is your RIR.

## Daily protein target
**Range:** **1.6–2.2 g per kg of bodyweight** (≈ 0.7–1.0 g per lb). On a cut, anchor to **goal or
lean bodyweight**, not current weight, and aim toward the top of the range to protect muscle.
**Distribute:** 3–4 meals of ~**0.4 g/kg** each (≈ 30–50 g protein per meal) hits the muscle-building
threshold each time.
**Find yours:** goal weight in lb × 0.8–1.0 = grams/day. (Goal 195 lb → ~155–195 g/day.)

## Calories: maintenance (TDEE) and deficit
**Maintenance (TDEE):** the calories that hold your weight steady. Estimate two ways —
1. **Track it:** log intake for 1–2 weeks at stable weight; that average ≈ maintenance.
2. **Formula:** Mifflin–St Jeor BMR × an activity factor (a calculator does this).
**Deficit for fat loss:** subtract **~300–500 kcal/day**, targeting **~0.5–1% of bodyweight per week**.
Going faster than ~1%/week costs muscle and tanks hormones — slower is smarter on a long cut.
**Verify:** re-check the scale trend every 1–2 weeks (weekly averages, not daily), adjust ~100–200 kcal.

## Caffeine cut-off time
**Why:** caffeine's half-life is **~5–6 hours**, so half is still in you that long after a dose, and
even caffeine taken **6 h before bed** measurably worsens sleep.
**Find yours:** stop caffeine **~8–10 h before bedtime**. (Bed 20:15 → last caffeine ~10:15–12:15.)
Slow metabolizers (jittery, sleep easily disrupted) should cut off earlier.

## Nightly sleep need
**Range:** most adults need **7–9 h**; athletes in hard training often the upper end.
**Find yours:** on 2–3 off days, sleep with no alarm and note the natural wake time and how many
hours that took; the point where daytime alertness is steady without caffeine is your need.
**Verify:** consistent wake time, minimal grogginess, stable afternoon energy.

## A note on how to read the plan
Each scheduled item is tagged **A / B / C** for how strong the *human* evidence is (A = systematic
review/guideline, B = randomized trial, C = animal/mechanism/weak). Prioritize the A/B items; treat
C items as optional experiments, not settled facts.
