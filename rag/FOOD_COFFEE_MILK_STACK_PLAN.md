- **Settled enough to operate:** during athlete weight loss, the retrieved expert literature converges on a gradual deficit, progressive resistance training, protein at 1.6–2.4 g/kg/day, and distribution across multiple high-quality-protein feedings. # 2. TARGETS

- **Protein — EVIDENCE AMOUNT:** **1.6–2.4 g/kg/day** during weight loss, commonly spread across **4–6 feeds** from high-quality sources. [A \| 07_supplements/general_ods_position_stands \| 10.1007/s40279-025-02285-4]
- **Protein — PLANNING AMOUNT, Phase A:** 200 lb = 90.7 kg; `90.7 × 1.6 = 145 g/day` and `90.7 × 2.4 = 218 g/day`. Use `90.7 × 1.8 = 163 g`, rounded to **165 g/day**, as the repeatable planning point inside the evidence range. This is not a claim that 1.8 g/kg is uniquely optimal.
- **Meal protein — EVIDENCE AMOUNT:** retrieved guidance commonly reports **20–40 g** or **0.4–0.5 g/kg per meal** and spreading protein through the day. ## Raw milk verdict

- A human brucellosis series found the disease strongly associated with consumption of unpasteurized dairy products; unpasteurized fresh cheese was reported by 79.4% of patients in that endemic-region series. [C \| 01_food_inflammation/raw_milk_safety \| 10.36519/idcm.2026.984]

[C \| 01_food_inflammation/raw_milk_safety \| 10.3389/fmicb.2026.1748367]
- The retrieved library provides no human evidence that raw milk improves fat loss, muscle retention, running, cognition, or tirzepatide tolerance more than pasteurized milk.
- **VERDICT: DO NOT DRINK RAW MILK.** The retrieved evidence shows avoidable pathogen exposure and no demonstrated body-composition advantage. [C \| 01_food_inflammation/raw_milk_safety \| 10.36519/idcm.2026.984] [C \| 01_food_inflammation/raw_milk_safety \| 10.3389/fmicb.2026.1748367]
